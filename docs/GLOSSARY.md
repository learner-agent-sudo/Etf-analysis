# Glossary — plain-English ETF terms

Definitions aimed at the buy/sell decision, not textbook completeness.

### Structure & cost
- **ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)** — a basket of securities trading like a single
  stock. You buy shares of the basket.
- **Expense ratio (TER)** — annual fee as a % of your investment, skimmed daily
  from assets. 0.50% on $10,000 = ~$50/yr, win or lose.
- **AUM (Assets Under Management)** — total money in the fund. Small AUM
  (< ~$50M) raises the risk the fund gets closed.
- **NAV (Net Asset Value)** — the true per-share value of the holdings. Market
  price can trade above (**premium**) or below (**discount**) NAV.
- **Bid/ask spread** — gap between buy and sell price; a hidden trading cost,
  wider on thinly-traded funds.

### Strategy
- **Passive / index fund** — mechanically tracks a published index. Low cost,
  transparent, no manager judgment.
- **Active fund** — a manager picks holdings, aiming to beat a benchmark. Higher
  fees; outcome depends on the manager.
- **Index methodology** — the rulebook a passive fund follows:
  - **Cap-weighted** — bigger companies get bigger weights (concentrates in
    mega-caps).
  - **Equal-weighted** — every holding gets the same weight (spreads risk, needs
    rebalancing).
  - **Tiered / modified** — hybrid rules.
- **Thematic ETF** — built around a story/trend (space, AI, pharma) rather than a
  standard sector or broad market.

### Options & leverage (the "strategy" funds)
- **Covered call** — the fund owns stocks and *sells call options* on them for
  income. Trade-off: you collect premium but **cap your upside** if the stock
  jumps.
- **Protective put / buffer** — options used to limit downside, usually also
  limiting upside. "Defined-outcome" funds work this way.
- **Yield (distribution rate)** — income paid out as a % of price. ⚠️ A very high
  yield can be **return of capital** (your own money handed back), not profit.
- **Leveraged ETF (2x/3x)** — amplifies *daily* moves with derivatives. Suffers
  **volatility decay** over time; designed for short holds, not buy-and-hold.
- **Inverse ETF** — rises when the underlying falls; also a short-term tool.

### Holdings & risk
- **Holdings** — the actual securities the fund owns.
- **Top-10 weight** — % of the fund in its 10 largest positions; a quick
  concentration gauge.
- **Concentration** — how much is riding on a few names. High concentration =
  higher single-stock risk.
- **Theme purity** *(this repo's term)* — how well the actual holdings match the
  fund's stated theme. Low purity = you're buying something other than the label.
- **Theme drift** *(this repo's term)* — when a fund quietly broadens its mandate
  (e.g. "space" → "space & defense"), changing what you own.
- **Sector exposure** — the industry breakdown of holdings (tech, industrials,
  healthcare, …).
- **Drawdown** — peak-to-trough drop. **Max drawdown** is the worst historical
  fall; a gut-check on how much pain the fund can inflict.
- **Volatility** — how much the price swings. Higher = bumpier ride.
- **Tracking difference / error** — for passive funds, how far returns stray from
  the index they promise to follow.

### Filings
- **Prospectus** — the fund's official legal document: strategy, risks, fees.
- **Fact sheet** — short issuer summary (usually monthly), the best quick read.
- **SEC EDGAR** — free US database of regulatory filings (`sec.gov`).
- **N-CSR** — annual/semi-annual shareholder report (audited holdings & financials).
- **485BPOS / 497K** — prospectus-related filings.
